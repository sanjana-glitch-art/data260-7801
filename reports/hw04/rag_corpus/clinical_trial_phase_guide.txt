Clinical Trial Phase Guide

Phase I clinical trials primarily evaluate safety, dosage, and side effects. They commonly involve a relatively small number of participants.

Phase II clinical trials continue evaluating safety while studying whether the intervention appears effective for its intended condition.

Phase III clinical trials compare the intervention with existing treatments, a placebo, or standard care. These studies commonly involve larger participant groups and may support regulatory review.

Phase IV studies occur after a treatment has received regulatory approval. They monitor long-term safety, effectiveness, uncommon adverse effects, and use in broader patient populations.

The clinical-trial listing application requires every record to have a trial title, sponsor name, submitter email, description, and trial phase. Valid phase values are Phase I, Phase II, Phase III, and Phase IV.

A trial phase describes a stage of research. It does not by itself prove that a treatment is safe, effective, approved, or appropriate for a particular patient.
